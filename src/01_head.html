Bike score — methodology and references

The tool's bike score follows Bike Score®, the bikeability measure developed by Walk Score with Meghan Winters (SFU), Michael Brauer and Kay Teschke (UBC) — the tool listed on UBC's Cycling in Cities page — and validated against cycling behaviour in 24 Canadian and US cities (Winters et al. 2016). Bike Score rates a place 0–100 from four equally weighted parts: bike lanes, hills, destinations and road connectivity, and bike-commute mode share. The tool reproduces the first three on a 100 m grid over Vancouver and leaves out mode share, which is a census outcome a design cannot change, so each remaining part weighs one third.

1 · Bike lanes (one third)

Bike Score sums nearby bikeway length with a distance decay (nothing beyond 1,000 m), counting paths at twice a painted lane and three times a shared route, and excludes sharrows. Here every bikeway in the City's layer (plus OpenStreetMap cycleways) is sampled every 25 m, weighted 3 for off-street paths and protected lanes, 1.5 for painted lanes, 1 for local-street bikeways and 0 for sharrows, and summed for each cell with a linear decay to zero at 1,000 m. Bike Score normalises "against an average of the highest scores"; the tool takes the mean of the top 2 % of cells as 100 and applies a square root, so the first lanes near a place count more than the tenth. A proposal adds its blocks to this part at the weight of the separation chosen for each block — a protected lane 3, flex posts 2 (an interim protected lane), a painted buffer 1.5, a shared local street bikeway 1 — less anything already there; so the score responds to the design, and the change is what its statistics report.

2 · Hills (one third)

Bike Score uses the steepest grade within 200 m, scoring 2 % as 100 and 10 % as 0. The tool computes grades from the City's 1 m contour lines on a 20 m grid (smoothed over 100 m, the grade a cyclist feels rather than a 20 m bump), takes the steepest within 200 m of each cell, and maps 2–10 % to 100–0. The proposal's own grades (average, steepest 20 m, metres over 5 %) are reported separately against the All Ages and Abilities (AAA) guidance of the BC Active Transportation Design Guide (keep under 3 %; 3–5 % acceptable for short lengths; over 5 % needs extra width).

3 · Destinations and connectivity (one third)

Bike Score uses "a modified Walk Score": the network distance to a diverse set of amenities and the road connectivity (intersection density, block length). The tool uses Walk Score's published category weights — grocery 3; restaurants and bars 10 slots (.75, .45, .25, .25, .225 × 4, .2 × 2); shopping 5 slots (.5, .45, .4, .35, .3); coffee 1.25 and .75; banks, parks, schools, libraries/bookshops and entertainment 1 each (15 points in all) — counting the nearest amenities first with Walk Score's own distance thresholds (full credit to 400 m, none beyond 1,600 m): the modification Bike Score applies is not published, and bicycle-scale distances saturate in a city this dense, so every cell would score the same. A Walk Score connectivity penalty of up to 5 % applies where there are fewer than 60 intersections per km² within 800 m, and up to 5 % where blocks average over 150 m. Amenities come from OpenStreetMap (8,281 places in the city); distances are straight-line, not network.

What the tool reports for a proposal

  • Bike score around the proposal: the mean over the grid cells within 400 m of its blocks, before (existing network) and after (with every proposal in the project), with the three parts.
  • Citywide: the mean over all scored cells before and after, and how many cells gain a point or more.
  • Connectivity: how many ends of the proposal meet the All Ages and Abilities (AAA) network, another facility, another block of the proposal, or nothing (a loose end).
  • Destinations: places within 400 m (a two-minute ride) by category.
  • Grades along the proposal, from the contour grid.

Departures from Bike Score, stated plainly

  • Mode share is left out (three parts, not four).
  • Distances to amenities are straight-line; Bike Score / Walk Score use network distance.
  • Walk Score's walking thresholds are used unmodified for amenities, and the square-root lane scaling is the tool's choice; both are documented in scripts/score.py and can be changed there.
  • Cells with no street within 150 m (water, port, park interiors) carry no score.
